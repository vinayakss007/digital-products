The whole flow, from one sheet

1

New Draft Invoice

Takes the next number (INV-0001, 0002, …) and sets the due date from your payment terms. No reusing an old number by accident.

2

Type line items plainly

One per line: Website design | 1 | 45000. Quantity and rate become the amount; tax and total follow.

3

Create PDF in Drive

A real invoice document — your address, their GSTIN, an itemised table, totals in lakh-grouped format — saved to an Invoices folder.

4

Email PDF to Client

Attaches it, states the amount and the due date, and marks the row Sent. Confirm dialog first, so nothing fires by mis-click.

5

Mark Row as Paid

Stamps the date, sets the status, and appends a row to your Payments log. Your books write themselves.

6

Refresh Cash-Flow Summary

Rebuilds the Cash Flow tab: collected, outstanding, and overdue split into 1–30, 31–60 and 60+ days.

Small things that matter when money is involved

🔢

Indian number formatting

₹1,41,600, not ₹141,600. Lakh grouping throughout, because the alternative makes clients do arithmetic and ask questions.

🔒

Nothing sends without a confirm

Email and stage changes ask first, and show you the amount before anything leaves.

🧱

Drafts don't pretend to be receivables

An unfinished invoice stays out of your outstanding total. Only what you actually sent counts as money owed.

🔢

Numbers that don't collide

Invoice numbering continues from the highest existing number, and a blank row or a stray header can't shift your columns out of sync.

🧪

Tested on the maths

92 automated checks cover totals, tax rounding, the overdue age bands, blank sheets and bad input — plus the shipped sample data end to end.

🔓

Edit anything

Columns are mapped in one config block at the top. Currency per invoice. The PDF is plain HTML — put your logo on it if you want.
